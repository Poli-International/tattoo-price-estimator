# Client Pricing Guide & Documentation

How this estimator works, what shapes studio pricing, and how to prepare for your consultation.

## 1. Purpose and Limitations

The Tattoo Price Estimator gives price ranges based on typical studio rates in nine markets: the UK, London, the US, Western Europe, Australia, Central & Eastern Europe, Canada, Brazil and Thailand. It helps you understand the variables that shape pricing before speaking with an artist.

This tool deliberately does not provide binding quotes or set studio prices. Tattooing is bespoke craftsmanship. Custom drawing, body anatomy, cover-ups, scar tissue, and artist schedules can only be assessed in person. Only the artist undertaking your tattoo can give you an authentic quote.

## 2. The Six Pricing Inputs

The estimator calculates ranges from six core variables, each reflecting studio time, materials, or technical demands:

### Geographic Region

Studio commercial overheads and prevailing rates vary by country and city. Central London and major US metropolitan centers operate at higher hourly baselines than regional towns.

### Tattoo Dimensions

Size establishes baseline chair time. A tiny tattoo takes under an hour, whereas a full sleeve or back piece spans dozens of hours across multiple appointments.

### Detail and Density

Minimal designs with open linework require far fewer needle passes than intricate geometric patterns, fine-line realism, micro-portraits, or saturated blackwork.

### Color Palette

Black and grey relies on single-pigment shading washes. Full color requires multiple distinct inks, frequent rinses, needle cartridge swaps, and dense packing, increasing total chair time.

### Anatomical Placement

Flat, muscular areas like the outer bicep allow steady stretching and relaxed posture. Curved or sensitive zones like ribs, neck, hands, or stomach require deliberate technical pacing, client breath pauses, and difficult positioning.

### Artist Seniority

Standard resident artists charge typical regional rates. Senior artists and specialists with high demand, international recognition, or distinctive styles command premium hourly or day rates.

## 3. How to Read the Estimated Range

Every estimate presents a low and high figure. This spread reflects realistic differences you encounter between studios:

- **What keeps a quote near the low end:** Pre-drawn flash designs, clean stencil placement on flat skin, straightforward single-sitting execution, and standard resident studio rates keep quotes near the lower boundary.
- **What pushes a quote toward the high end:** Custom freehand drawing, dense color blends, difficult skin texture, cover-up work, slower pacing for client comfort, and specialist artists push quotes toward the upper boundary.

## 4. The Studio Shop Minimum

Every professional studio enforces a shop minimum price, even for a piece taking ten minutes. This is not an arbitrary charge.

Every procedure requires an identical sterile setup: medical-grade barrier film, surface disinfectants, single-use needle cartridges, ink caps, grip wraps, and autoclave sterilization. Station preparation and sanitization take 20 to 30 minutes of labor and medical consumables before needle work begins. The minimum ensures hospital-grade hygiene remains standard for every client.

## 5. Deposits and Booking Commitments

Professional studios universally require a booking deposit to reserve appointment time on the artist calendar.

Deposits reserve dedicated studio time and compensate custom drawing work done before your session. Deposits are non-refundable if you cancel without adequate notice, but are customarily deducted from the final price on the day of your tattoo.

## 6. Multi-Session Projects

Large projects like sleeves, chest panels, and back pieces cannot be completed safely in one sitting. They are split into distinct stages: outline, background shading, and final color saturation.

Studios space sessions 3 to 6 weeks apart to allow dermal healing and tissue recovery. Multi-session work is almost always billed per sitting or by day rate, spreading the cost across several months.

## 7. Four Questions to Bring to Your Consultation

Bringing specific questions to your consultation establishes clear expectations between you and the artist:

- **1. Pricing Structure**: Do you price this project as a flat quote, by the hour, or by full-day sittings?
- **2. Time Expectations**: How many total hours and sittings do you anticipate this piece will take?
- **3. Deposit and Touch-up Policy**: What is your deposit amount, cancellation notice window, and are touch-ups included after healing?
- **4. Placement and Scaling Recommendations**: Based on my concept and anatomy, would adjusting the scale or flow improve longevity?

## 8. Tipping Etiquette and Conventions

Tipping practices vary by country. Tipping is never mandatory, but understanding regional customs helps you navigate payment with confidence:

- **United States**: Tipping is customary and widely expected. Satisfied clients typically tip 15% to 20% of the session cost, given directly to the artist in cash.
- **United Kingdom**: Tipping is appreciated but strictly optional. Clients receiving custom artwork or attentive care often tip 10% or round up the bill.
- **Western Europe**: Tipping is modest and discretionary. Rounding up or giving 5% to 10% for custom work is common practice.
- **Australia**: Studio rates reflect full fair compensation. Tipping is not expected, though small discretionary gratuities for long sittings are accepted.

## 9. Using the Printable Consultation Worksheet

This tool includes a single-page consultation worksheet formatted for physical printing or PDF export. It summarizes your selected dimensions, anatomical placement, detail options, estimated price range, and consultation questions.

Bring the printed worksheet to your consultation. It gives you a clear visual agenda to discuss your ideas and pricing with your tattoo artist with clarity.
